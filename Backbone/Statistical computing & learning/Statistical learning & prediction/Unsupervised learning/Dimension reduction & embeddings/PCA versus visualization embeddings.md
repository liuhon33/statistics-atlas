# PCA versus visualization embeddings

Statistical computing & learning → Statistical learning & prediction → Unsupervised learning → Dimension reduction & embeddings → PCA versus visualization embeddings

Parent: [[Backbone/Statistical computing & learning/Statistical learning & prediction/Unsupervised learning/Dimension reduction & embeddings/Dimension reduction & embeddings|Dimension reduction & embeddings]]

## 1. See it and use it

Suppose each cell has thousands of gene measurements and you want a 2D plot. Every method must discard something; the useful question is **which relationship should survive?**

![[assets/figures/2026-11-11-pca/05-three-objectives.svg]]

*Original schematic of three objectives, not a performance ranking.*

[[Backbone/Statistical inference & modeling/Multivariate analysis/Multivariate statistical analysis/Linear dimension reduction/Principal component analysis|PCA]] finds shared linear directions that retain variance and minimize squared reconstruction error. Its axes have explicit feature weights.

**t-SNE** fits coordinates to match pairwise similarity probabilities. **UMAP** fits coordinates to represent a weighted neighborhood graph. These nonlinear embeddings emphasize neighborhood relationships; their axes are not PC scores or weighted gene combinations. Definitions: [van der Maaten & Hinton, §3](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume9/vandermaaten08a/vandermaaten08a.pdf) and [UMAP, “The UMAP Algorithm”](https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/how_umap_works.html#the-umap-algorithm).

For example, first compress 3,000 features to 50 PCs, then embed those 50 coordinates in 2D. The first step makes a smaller linear representation; the second makes a neighborhood display. Compare several settings, and check apparent groups against the higher-dimensional data. A separated island alone does not establish a cell type, a classifier, or a quantitative distance between groups.
