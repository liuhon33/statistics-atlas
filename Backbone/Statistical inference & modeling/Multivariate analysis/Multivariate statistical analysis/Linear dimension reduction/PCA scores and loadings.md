# PCA scores and loadings

Statistical inference & modeling → Multivariate analysis → Multivariate statistical analysis → Linear dimension reduction → PCA scores and loadings

Parent: [[Backbone/Statistical inference & modeling/Multivariate analysis/Multivariate statistical analysis/Linear dimension reduction/Linear dimension reduction|Linear dimension reduction]]

## 1. See it and use it

After PCA, one table describes the **features**, and another describes the **samples**. Mixing them up changes what a plot means.

![[assets/figures/2026-11-11-pca/04-scores-loadings.svg]]

*Original illustration using the twelve samples in [[Backbone/Statistical inference & modeling/Multivariate analysis/Multivariate statistical analysis/Linear dimension reduction/Principal component analysis|the PCA example]]. Two feature weights produce twelve different sample scores.*

**Loadings** here mean the unit direction coefficients: the weights used to combine centered features. **Scores** are each sample's coordinates along those directions. For centered $X_c$ with samples in rows and loading columns in $V$, the score table is $T=X_cV$. [Stanford STATS 305C, “Back to reduced rank” and “PCA = SVD”](https://web.stanford.edu/class/stats305c/slides/PCA_I.html).

> [!tip] SVD: $V$ gives loadings; $U\Sigma$ gives scores
> With **samples in rows and features in columns**, write the compact SVD $X_c=U\Sigma V^\top$. Its columns satisfy $V^\top V=I$, so
> $$
> \underbrace{T}_{\text{sample scores}}
> =X_c\underbrace{V}_{\text{feature loadings}}
> =U\Sigma\underbrace{(V^\top V)}_{I}
> =U\Sigma.
> $$
> - **$v_j$** (column $j$ of $V$): the feature weights defining PC$j$ — its **loadings**.
> - **$\sigma_j u_j$** (column $j$ of $U\Sigma$): all samples' **scores** on PC$j$.
> - **$u_j$ alone**: that score vector divided by $\sigma_j>0$, hence normalized to unit length — **not the actual scores**.
>
> This is exactly $X_cv_j=\sigma_j u_j$ in [[Backbone/Probability & mathematical foundations/Mathematical tools/Linear algebra for statistics/Matrices & decompositions/Singular value decomposition#Why this gives PCA|the SVD–PCA explanation]].

For PC1 above, both weights are $1/\sqrt2$. Centered readings $(2,2)$ yield score $2\sqrt2$; readings $(-2,-2)$ yield its negative. The weights stay fixed while the sample changes.

A **score plot** compares samples; a **loading plot** shows how features enter components. In Python these are `fit_transform(X)` and `components_.T`; in R they are `fit$x` and `fit$rotation`. Some references instead call feature–score correlations “loadings,” so check the convention. Signs may reverse together without changing the PCA reconstruction.
