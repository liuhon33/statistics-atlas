## Corrections and qualifications

- The p. 1 ANOVA labels/projectors are not consistently matched to their degrees of freedom. With three observations, a full intercept-plus-slope model has **one residual degree of freedom**, the intercept-only model has **two**, and the reduction has **one**. The checked table identifies each quantity by its projector and rank.
- A plane in response space has dimension two but lives in $\mathbb R^3$ in the picture. Its residual dimension is the complementary dimension, not the fitted dimension. Coefficient coordinates are also distinct from response-vector directions.
- Gaussian **scalar coordinates along orthonormal directions** are independent normals. Lengths of projected vectors are nonnegative; their squared lengths, divided by $\sigma^2$, have chi-square laws. The vector components themselves do not all have the same full-dimensional normal distribution.
- The red p. 2 correction correctly restores $\arctan\sqrt{u}$ in the squared-Cauchy CDF. The notes also already contain $\sqrt{161.45}$ in the interval. These correct steps are retained; the multiplier is approximately $12.706$, while $161.45$ is the F critical value.
- Exact Gaussian regression uses a t critical value with the residual degrees of freedom. Replacing it by 1.96 is a large-degrees-of-freedom approximation; a larger t critical value does not require nonnormal observation errors.
- “Single coefficient versus several joint restrictions” determines the t/F distinction. A coefficient t test is valid within a multiple-predictor regression. For one matching restriction, the F statistic equals the squared t statistic.
- A small p-value is a small tail probability **under the null model**, not a posterior probability that the null is true. The notes' correct repeated-sampling interpretation is retained, and nearby informal probability-of-the-null wording is clarified.
- For a fixed candidate slope $b$, the null fit is an affine line: its intercept varies while $b$ stays fixed. The derivation now distinguishes these two roles.

These are mathematical checks and agent-added clarifications, not invented additional user questions.
